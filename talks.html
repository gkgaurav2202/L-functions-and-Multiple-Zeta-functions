Maki Nakasuji Duality for Shifted Multi-Indexed Poly-Bernoulli Numbers Sophia University, Tokyo, Japan

Day and time: 15th December, 2026 2:30 pm

Poly-Bernoulli numbers were introduced by Kaneko as a generalization of classical Bernoulli numbers. One of their remarkable properties is the duality formula for negative indices, which was later extended to multi-indexed poly-Bernoulli numbers by Kaneko and Tsumura. In this talk, we give an alternative proof of this duality formula using explicit formulas involving Stirling numbers of the second kind. We also discuss periodicity properties of multi-indexed poly-Bernoulli numbers. We then introduce shifted multi-indexed poly-Bernoulli numbers and establish a duality formula and periodicity congruences for them. Furthermore, we derive an arbitrary-depth product formula expressing these numbers in terms of products of depth-one shifted poly-Bernoulli numbers. The first part of this talk is based on joint work with Yuna Baba, Tomoko Kikuchi, and Mika Sakata. The second part is based on ongoing joint work with Herbert Gangl and Koji Tasaka.

Masatoshi Suzuki On detecting zeros of Dirichlet L-functions from the Riemann zeta-function Institute of Science Tokyo, Japan

Day and time: 16th December, 2026 10:50 am

Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, we can use a weighted finite sum over the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta-function to obtain a criterion for the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. Under the corresponding hypotheses, the same sum also allows us to determine the multiplicities of individual zeros of these L-functions. In this talk, we focus on the ideas behind the proof. We evaluate a sum involving the von Mangoldt function in two different ways. Using Dirichlet characters and Perron’s formula, we explain how the multiplicities of zeros appear. On the other hand, an explicit formula for the Riemann zeta-function and the method of stationary phase explain the particular form of the weighted sum. Comparing the two evaluations gives the desired results.

Koji Tasaka Unified multiple L-functions and their values at integer points Kindai University, Osaka, Japan

Day and time: 15th December, 2026 4:35 pm

The Kaneko–Zagier conjecture asserts a correspondence between finite multiple zeta values and symmetric multiple zeta values. A higher-level version of this conjecture, involving twists by roots of unity, has also been formulated. The speaker previously studied this higher-level version using multiple harmonic q-series. In this talk, we discuss the conjecture from a different perspective, namely through a higher-level generalization of Komori’s unified multiple zeta functions. We show that the unified multiple L-functions are entire, and by examining their values at integer points, we show that the higher-level Kaneko–Zagier conjecture for general integer indices reduces to the case of positive indices. This talk is based on joint work with P. S. Mehta and B. Saha (IIT Delhi).
